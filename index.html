01 — The Dastorkon

No traveller leaves a yurt hungry.

On the high pastures of Kyrgyzstan, a guest is a gift. The moment you arrive, a cloth is spread on the floor — the dastorkon — and it fills with bread, tea, dumplings and whatever the herd and the season allow.

Nomad Manti brings that table to Roosevelt. We're a small, family-run kitchen cooking the food we grew up with. Our little building on NE 65th, a former bus stop, now wears the reds, golds and blues of a shyrdak felt carpet — and inside, the kitchen is open, the dough is fresh, and every entrée comes with bread and black tea or a cold drink.

See what's on the cloth today

01

Folded by hand

Each manti is rolled, filled with lamb and onion and pinched shut by hand, then steamed until the dough turns silk-thin.

02

Fire & smoke

Every Friday and Saturday the charcoal is lit: marinated lamb on flat steel skewers, grilled until juicy with a whisper of smoke.

03

The wok of the Silk Road

Boso lagman — homemade noodles tossed hard with beef, bell peppers, celery, green beans and garlic. Our guests' favourite, by a wide margin.
